I am pleased to announce that our current development release, Ubuntu Precise, is *now open for translation*

[![Translate Ubuntu!](/content/images/2010/12/translateubuntubutton.png)](https://translations.launchpad.net/ubuntu)

Some additional information that will be useful for translators:

- **Translation schedule**. Remember that according to the [release schedule](https://wiki.ubuntu.com/PrecisePangolin/ReleaseSchedule) translatable messages might be subject to change until the [User Interface Freeze](https://wiki.ubuntu.com/UserInterfaceFreeze) on the week of the **23rd of February**.
- **Language packs**. During the development cycle, language packs containing translations will be released [twice per week](https://dev.launchpad.net/Translations/LanguagePackSchedule) except for the freeze periods. This will allow users and translators to quickly see and test the results of translations.
- **Test and report bugs**. If you notice any issues (e.g. untranslated strings or applications), do check with the [translation team for your language](https://translations.launchpad.net/+groups/ubuntu-translators) first. If you think it is a genuine bug, please [report it](https://bugs.launchpad.net/ubuntu-translations/+filebug).
- **Learn more**. [Learn how to start translating Ubuntu](https://wiki.ubuntu.com/Translations/) and enable millions to use it in their language.

**Ubuntu 12.04** will be a **Long Term Support** release, so let's rally around translations to provide the best translated OS around and go over the mark of [nearly 40 languages in which Ubuntu is fully translated](http://people.canonical.com/~dpm/stats/ubuntu-11.10-translation-stats.html)!
